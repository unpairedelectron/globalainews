## LinkedIn (≤120 words)

At the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety, the UN asked member states to build a multilateral AI risk framework within a year, with independent oversight.

The interesting part is capacity, not rules. Citing the World Development Report 2026, the UN's envoy said high-income countries have 500 incident response teams and low-income countries six. "These AI risks don't recognize borders."

The item with most bite for India: the UN-backed panel's work on evaluation capacity, so systems are safe "across languages and cultures". A panel member said models "perform far worse and can be less safe" outside English.

That is a safety claim, not a quality one. English-only evaluation is not evidence of safety for your users.

## Telegram

**The UN's Bridgetown Call to Action on AI**

Barbados, 23 Nov, co-hosted by the UN and PM Mia Mottley.

**The ask:** a globally inclusive, principles-based multilateral framework for managing AI risk, within a year, with independent and credible oversight. Plus early pledges to a proposed Global Fund for AI Capacity-Building.

**The honest caveat**, from the UN's own Special Envoy: "It's not a treaty, it's not regulation, but it is a framework which different countries can then use to build up their own national AI safety infrastructure."

**The number:** 500 incident response teams in high-income countries, six in low-income ones (World Development Report 2026, cited by the envoy). Existing UN-backed network: 27 centres of excellence across five regions.

**For India:** the panel's finding that models "perform far worse and can be less safe" outside English, and that the fix is countries testing in their own languages. India has 22 scheduled languages.

**Also:** Mottley wants a contractual kill switch — "It has to be a condition of the sale that what I'm buying from you, I'm capable of saying, that's it. No more."

Guterres leaves office at the end of December, so delivery falls to his successor.
