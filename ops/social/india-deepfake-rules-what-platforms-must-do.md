## LinkedIn (≤120 words)

India's synthetic-media rules are widely summarised. They are also widely summarised wrong.

Read the actual definition in rule 2(1)(wa) and it covers "audio, visual or audio-visual information". AI-generated text falls outside it — even though government descriptions of the deepfake problem mention text.

Four deadlines tightened on 10 February 2026: three hours to act on a court order or an authorised government intimation, two hours on a complaint about morphed intimate imagery, 36 hours for general removal requests, seven days for ordinary grievances.

And the penalty for failing is not a fine. An intermediary that "knowingly permitted, promoted, or failed to act upon" such content is deemed to have failed due diligence — which is the condition for safe harbour.

## Telegram

**India's deepfake rules: what platforms must actually do**

Amended IT Rules, notification G.S.R. 120(E), 10 February 2026.

**The four clocks** (all shortened):
• 3 hours — court order or reasoned government intimation (was 36)
• 2 hours — morphed intimate imagery or impersonation complaints (was 24)
• 36 hours — general removal requests (was 72)
• 7 days — ordinary grievances (was 15)

**If you build a generator:** block four categories outright; label everything else prominently and embed permanent metadata with a unique identifier, and don't let anyone strip it.

**If you're above 50 lakh users:** ask uploaders to declare synthetic content, verify the declaration with technical measures, label what's confirmed.

**Two details worth knowing:** the definition covers audio and images, not text. And a three-hour takedown notice must name the legal basis and the specific URL, with a monthly Secretary-level proportionality review.

Context: the appellate committee's portal showed 85,381 appeals received and 18,439 disposed on 24 Nov.
