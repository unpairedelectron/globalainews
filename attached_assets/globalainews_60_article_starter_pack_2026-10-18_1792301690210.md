## Mandatory editorial rules for Replit

- Do not invent or extrapolate facts, quotes, statistics, dates, research findings, funding terms, model capabilities or policy status.
- Use linked sources as reporting inputs, not as text to copy. Write original prose and cite factual claims inline.
- Before publication, re-open each source; verify the current status, relevant date, source type, geography, and exact numbers.
- Distinguish company claims from independent findings, proposals from enacted law, reported signals from established outcomes, and opinions from news.
- For Brief, refresh the 24-hour window at publication and discard any entry that falls outside it; all six seeds were selected from material reported on 17–18 October 2026 at research time.
- Expand short starters into 500–800 word articles only when enough reporting evidence supports that length. Never pad with generic paragraphs.
- For Research and Data, link original papers, repositories, model cards or product docs when available; note peer-review and dataset-release status precisely.
- Use author and reviewer metadata only when verified by the publisher. Do not fabricate credentials, quotes, interviews, image rights or first-hand reporting.
- Add a clear update date only after a substantive update; do not reuse the current date as a false original publication date.
- Avoid duplicate story angles across sections. Where a source is reused for contextual analysis, add a genuinely different editorial question and state the relationship.
- Do not publish health, legal, cybersecurity or financial guidance as personalized advice. Add appropriate expert context and limitations.
- Create no images implying real events or real people unless authentic and rights-cleared. Use original diagrams/data visualizations or clearly labelled illustrations.
