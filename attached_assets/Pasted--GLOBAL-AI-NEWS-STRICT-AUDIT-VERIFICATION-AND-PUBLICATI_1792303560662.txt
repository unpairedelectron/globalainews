## 2. Establish a strict publication gate

Every article must pass all applicable checks below before it can be published.

An article failing a mandatory check must remain unpublished. Fix the underlying problem wherever possible; otherwise, mark the article as blocked and document the exact reason.

**Do not lower standards to achieve a target of 18 published articles.**

The goal is to publish all 18 if the evidence supports them. Accuracy takes precedence over the count.

### Gate A — source and claim verification

Open and inspect each supplied source URL. Do not rely exclusively on the existing starter text, search-result snippets, AI summaries or the fact that an article has already been drafted.

For every material factual claim, verify:

- The precise claim made.
- The source that directly supports it.
- The original publication date and relevant event date.
- Whether the source is primary research, an official announcement, company marketing, independent reporting or editorial interpretation.
- Exact figures, units, comparisons and time periods.
- Whether the work is a preprint, peer-reviewed paper, technical report, benchmark, product announcement or research demonstration.
- Whether a dataset, API, repository, model, benchmark or product is actually available.
- Applicable access restrictions, licensing terms and reproducibility limitations.

Use official announcements, original papers, repositories, model cards, technical documentation and relevant government or institutional sources whenever available.

Use reliable independent reporting to corroborate material claims where appropriate.

If the original source is paywalled, unavailable or too vague to support an important claim, seek a verifiable alternative source. Do not pretend the inaccessible source was fully reviewed.

Maintain a source-verification record for each article containing the source URL, source type, verified claims, relevant dates, evidence notes and unresolved claims.

### Gate B — factual accuracy and editorial integrity

Do not invent or extrapolate:

- Research findings, scientific results or benchmark rankings.
- Model capabilities or performance improvements.
- Funding figures, commercial terms or product prices.
- Quotes, interviews or statements.
- Sample sizes, percentages or methodology details.
- Government actions, legislation or regulatory status.
- Dataset contents, download availability or licensing conditions.
- Authors, reviewers, credentials, publication dates or first-hand reporting.
- Image provenance, copyright permissions or visual evidence.

Clearly distinguish a company's own claims from independent evidence. Label opinions and analysis appropriately.

If an article asserts that a model or dataset outperforms an alternative, require a valid comparison and supporting evidence.

If a result is preliminary, say so. If a study is observational, do not imply causation. If scientific work has not undergone peer review, do not imply that it has.

For health-related AI research, do not present experimental results as medical advice or approved clinical practice.

### Gate C — article quality and storytelling

Review every draft from beginning to end.

Each article must contain:

1. A clear, distinctive headline.
2. A compelling opening that introduces the problem, discovery or development.
3. The relevant context and verified facts.
4. A substantive explanation of why the development matters.
5. Limitations, uncertainty or counterevidence where appropriate.
6. A clear distinction between known facts and interpretation.
7. A conclusion that follows from the evidence.
8. Appropriate inline source links.
9. Useful subheadings and readable paragraphs.
10. An accurate section assignment.

Remove generic filler, repeated paragraphs, unsupported predictions, exaggerated language and AI-generated boilerplate.

Do not copy the wording of external sources. Produce original editorial prose while preserving the meaning of the underlying evidence.

For expanded research and data articles, aim for the established 500–800-word target where the evidence supports that length. Do not pad articles simply to meet a word count. Maintain the concise-news format for Brief if required by the live product's editorial specification.

### Gate D — uniqueness and duplication

Check all 18 articles against:

- One another.
- Existing published Global AI News content.
- Existing drafts and scheduled articles.
- Titles and slugs already in the CMS.
- Substantially similar story angles.
- Repeated paragraphs or duplicated content.

Every article must have a unique title and slug.

Avoid publishing different versions of the same press release across Brief, Research and Data without a genuinely different editorial question and appropriate cross-referencing.

If two articles substantially overlap, rewrite them to serve distinct reader needs or retain the stronger version and block the duplicate.

Do not create duplicate CMS records during retries or repeated imports.

### Gate E — SEO and metadata

For every article, verify:

- Correct newsroom section and category.
- Unique, readable, permanent slug.
- Accurate SEO title.
- Accurate meta description.
- Natural primary and secondary keywords.
- One clear primary headline and logical H2/H3 structure.
- Correct canonical URL.
- Relevant internal links.
- Accurate source attribution.
- Relevant and rights-cleared image, if used.
- Meaningful image alt text.
- Correct article schema and publisher metadata.

Metadata must reflect the actual article. Do not insert unsupported claims into titles or descriptions to improve click-through rate.

Check the rendered HTML, not just the JSON fields.

Use accurate author metadata already established in the project. Never invent an author biography, credentials, reviewer or expert endorsement.

Do not fabricate an original publication date. Set the actual CMS publication timestamp when an article is genuinely published. Preserve existing, verified original publication dates where appropriate.

### Gate F — Brief section freshness

Brief has an additional time-sensitive requirement: each story must qualify as relevant AI news from the **24 hours preceding publication**.

For every Brief article:

1. Recheck the original source and timestamp immediately before publication.
2. Confirm the relevant event or reporting falls inside the allowed 24-hour window.
3. Distinguish publication time from the event's actual date.
4. Confirm that the development has not been superseded or materially corrected.
5. Update the copy if subsequent reporting changes the facts.

If a story is too old, replace it with a verified, relevant development from the current window if a suitable source can be found. Preserve a unique title and slug and update its sources accordingly.

If no suitable replacement can be verified, leave that slot unpublished. Never label an older development as breaking news.

Recheck the current time at execution; do not rely on the batch creation date alone.

### Gate G — Data-section accuracy

Because Batch 3 focuses on datasets, benchmarks and AI data products, explicitly verify:

- Whether the resource is publicly downloadable or merely described in a blog post.
- Whether the API is available to all users, requires allowlisting, requires credentials or has restricted access.
- Whether a benchmark has been independently validated or is only a publisher's own evaluation.
- Whether the underlying data are open, licensed, access-controlled or unavailable.
- What the dataset or benchmark actually measures.
- Whether its licence permits the described use.
- Whether examples, reported numbers and access terms are current.

Do not describe research collaborations as publicly released datasets unless the actual artifacts are accessible.

Do not describe benchmark results as an industry standard or independent certification without evidence.

### Gate H — Research-section accuracy

For Batch 2, verify the original research page or repository for each story.

Check whether:

- The stated findings match the paper or research report.
- The reported performance improvement uses the correct baseline and metric.
- The methodology and evaluation conditions are accurately explained.
- Limitations and unresolved questions are included.
- A proof has actually been formally verified if the article describes it as verified.
- A scientific manuscript is peer-reviewed if the article claims that it is.
- Company-reported experiments are attributed correctly.

Where formal verification is partial, clearly describe its scope. Where an experiment has not been independently replicated, say so.

### Gate I — Trust and public-publisher requirements

Check the final public page for:

- Accurate bylines and dates.
- Working source links.
- No placeholder author, reviewer, citation or image records.
- No broken or misleading page elements.
- No unsupported medical, security, financial or legal recommendations.
- Correct publisher identity and editorial metadata.
- Appropriate correction and contact mechanisms consistent with the existing site.

Do not assume that passing this gate guarantees Google News inclusion, AdSense approval or search rankings.
