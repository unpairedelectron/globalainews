## India ‘AI Impact Summit 2026’ to focus on democratisation of AI

![India ‘AI Impact Summit 2026’ to focus on democratisation of AI](https://ddnews.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/AI-impact-summit.jpg)

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a central pillar of India’s development journey, strengthening governance, improving public service delivery and enabling solutions that can reach citizens at scale. Just as electricity, computers, the internet and mobile phones reshaped human progress, AI is now working alongside people to transform sectors such as agriculture, healthcare, education, manufacturing, climate action and governance.

For India, the democratisation of AI is essential to ensure that these benefits are widely shared and aligned with the vision of Viksit Bharat by 2047. This approach places equitable access to computing power, data repositories and AI models at the heart of national strategy, enabling participation by startups, researchers, public institutions and innovators across regions.

Building on this vision, the India–AI Impact Summit 2026 will be held from February 16 to 20 at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi. As the first global AI summit hosted in the Global South, it will bring together global leaders, policymakers, technology companies and experts to deliberate on AI’s role in inclusive growth, governance and sustainable development.

**What does democratisation of AI mean**

Democratisation of AI refers to making artificial intelligence accessible, affordable and usable for a wide and diverse group of users. It goes beyond access to finished applications and includes access to core building blocks such as computing power, datasets and model ecosystems. When these resources are available at scale, individuals and institutions are able to innovate, compete and govern more effectively in the digital economy.

This approach also widens economic opportunity. India’s technology and AI ecosystem employs over six million people, while a NITI Aayog report released in October 2025 highlights AI’s potential to empower nearly 490 million informal workers by expanding access to services, markets and financial systems.

India’s AI journey builds on its proven digital philosophy. UPI democratised digital payments, Aadhaar enabled population-scale digital identity, and indigenous 4G and 5G stacks strengthened technological self-reliance. AI now follows the same path, guided by openness, affordability and accessibility.

**Democratising AI applications for public impact**

AI creates real value only when it reaches people at scale. India’s approach prioritises practical deployment across sectors so that AI improves everyday life and public services.

In agriculture, AI supports farmers by predicting weather patterns, identifying pest risks and guiding irrigation and sowing decisions. Platforms such as Kisan e-Mitra, the National Pest Surveillance System and Crop Health Monitoring tools use satellite and weather data to protect crops and improve income security.

In healthcare, AI enables early disease detection, supports medical image analysis and strengthens telemedicine services, improving access and quality of care in rural and remote areas.

Bhashini, an AI-powered language platform, enables translation and speech services across Indian languages, allowing citizens to access digital services without literacy barriers. Since its launch in July 2022, it has crossed 1.2 million downloads, supports 36+ languages, integrates over 350 AI models, and serves more than 450 active users.

AI is also strengthening disaster preparedness. The India Meteorological Department uses AI for rainfall, fog and extreme weather forecasting, while tools such as the Advanced Dvorak Technique support cyclone assessment. MausamGPT is being developed to offer real-time advisories.

India’s innovation landscape reflects this widespread adoption. As of January 2026, India ranks among the world’s top three startup ecosystems, with over two lakh startups, nearly 90 per cent of which use AI in some form.

**Democratising access to AI infrastructure**

Ensuring access to foundational AI infrastructure is critical for democratisation. Approved in March 2024 with an outlay of ₹10,371.92 crore over five years, the IndiaAI Mission is expanding access to compute, data and responsible AI usage for public good.

**Access to data and models**

High-quality datasets and reusable models are essential for AI innovation. AIKosh, India’s national AI dataset and model platform, aggregates resources from government and non-government sources across sectors.

As of February 2026, AIKosh hosts 7,541 datasets and 273 AI models across 20 sectors, with over 3.85 lakh visits, 11,000 registered users and 26,000 downloads.

India is also developing large multimodal AI models trained on Indian data and languages to ensure relevance and self-reliance. Under the IndiaAI Mission, 12 startups and institutions, including Sarvam AI, Gnani AI, BharatGen (IIT Bombay), Fractal Analytics and Tech Mahindra Maker’s Lab, have been selected for model development.

**Access to compute**

To overcome traditional barriers to computing power, India has onboarded over 38,000 high-end GPUs, available at ₹65 per hour, nearly one-third of the global average cost. Additionally, 1,050 TPUs have been made available to expand AI processing capacity.

Under the National Supercomputing Mission, over 40 petaflops of computing capacity has been deployed across IITs, IISERs and research institutions, supporting applications such as language processing, weather prediction and drug discovery.

**Chips, data centres and connectivity**

India is strengthening its semiconductor ecosystem through the India Semiconductor Mission, with an outlay of ₹76,000 crore. As of December 2025, 10 projects across six states had been approved, with cumulative investments of ₹1.60 lakh crore. The Union Budget 2026–27 announced India Semiconductor Mission 2.0, with a provision of ₹1,000 crore to support research, training and workforce development.

On connectivity, 5G services now cover 99.9 per cent of districts, reaching 85 per cent of the population. India’s data centre capacity stands at 1,280 MW and is expected to grow four to five times by 2030, supported by rising cloud adoption and AI usage.

**Education, skilling and AI literacy**

Democratisation of AI depends as much on people as on technology. India is investing in AI education from school to advanced research through Centres of Excellence, skilling programmes and fellowships.

Initiatives such as Skilling for AI Readiness (SOAR), YUVAi, vocational training under the Craftsmen Training Scheme, and fellowships under the IndiaAI Mission are building a future-ready workforce. Support is being extended to 500 PhD scholars, 5,000 postgraduates and 8,000 undergraduates, with AI labs being established in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities.

**Global cooperation for democratising AI**

Democratising AI globally requires coordinated action, particularly for countries in the Global South. The India–AI Impact Summit 2026 will bring together Heads of Government, ministers and global industry leaders to advance this agenda.

A key initiative is the Democratising AI Resources Working Group, co-chaired by India, Egypt and Kenya, which focuses on making AI resources accessible and affordable as global public goods, fostering international cooperation and supporting capacity building.

India’s approach to democratising AI demonstrates that scale, inclusion and innovation can advance together. By focusing on affordability, openness and trust, India is ensuring that AI benefits farmers, students, startups, researchers and public institutions alike.

As India hosts the India–AI Impact Summit 2026, it offers a Global South–led model of AI development rooted in equity and sustainability. Democratising AI is not a one-time initiative, but a continuing commitment to ensure that technological progress strengthens societies, reduces inequalities and supports sustainable development for all.
