# Current-source review — 3 November 2026

This is a follow-up to the ten claim-level fact logs prepared on 31 October. Existing claims and source URLs were retained. The review rechecked the time-sensitive primary records and spot-checked primary-source support for the central claims; it did not independently reopen every secondary link in all ten bibliographies.

## Results

1. **AI agent access and credentials** — The current 1Password, Google Cloud, Chrome and Opera pages support the product capabilities described in the draft: approved/brokered sign-in, a dedicated agent identity, Gmail-based autofill suggestions, and local browser AI. Keep these as vendor-described features, not proof of an independent security audit or a guarantee of safe permissions.
2. **Workforce announcements** — Microsoft’s 26 October statement still supports the voluntary exit offer for eligible employees in participating organizations. The Dutch government’s 26 October release supports an estimated 25,000-FTE reduction over several years and separately describes AI productivity work. Neither primary statement attributes a defined number of departures to AI. Keep Reuters’ 60,000 figure attributed and distinguish eligibility from completed departures.
3. **Anthropic S-1 governance** — The SEC filed Amendment No. 1 (S-1/A) on 2 November. It retains the proposed Class T board-majority and Class F 50.1% voting terms, and adds financial statements through 30 September. The article’s H1 figures remain historically supported by the 26 October S-1, but that is no longer the latest filing; the article and its timeline were updated to identify the S-1/A as current and keep H1 figures period-labeled. No offering date or final price/share count is set in the latest filing.
4. **EU AI Act deadlines** — The official amending regulation and Commission timeline continue to describe staggered application dates, including 2 December 2027 for the relevant Annex III high-risk systems and 2 August 2028 for relevant Annex I systems. The 2 December 2026 dates remain tied to the specified Article 5 additions and the Article 50(2) transition; do not generalize them to all AI Act duties.
5. **Gemini 4 Carbon** — Google DeepMind’s launch and methodology pages support the reported SEC-bench Pro, ExploitGym and CWE-bench figures. The methodology distinguishes Google-computed results from public-leaderboard results. Retain attribution, test-specific limitations and the distinction between benchmark scores and general safety.
6. **GitHub Octoverse** — GitHub’s 2026 report supports the September “more than 40%” and March “17%” agent-started pull-request figures. These remain GitHub’s platform-specific first-draft detection measure, not a measure of every AI-assisted pull request or of quality.
7. **Hyperscaler capital expenditure** — The current Q3/Q1 company releases and calls support the reported Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon figures. Keep each company’s period and accounting basis attached: Amazon cash CapEx, Meta lease-inclusive CapEx, Alphabet guidance, and Microsoft calendar-year estimate versus fiscal-quarter actual.
8. **IndiaAI compute tender** — C-DAC’s 27 October corrigendum still lists bid submission ending 13 November 2026 at 3:30 p.m. and opening on 14 November at 4 p.m. IST. The RFE is a request for up to 10,000 AI compute units, not evidence of awards, installed capacity or usage.
9. **India AI rules and consultation** — The Gazette remains the controlling source for G.S.R. 120(E), dated 10 February 2026. The current search of official MeitY/PIB sources did not locate the anticipated AI-governance consultation paper; a separate PIB notice about an IT/BPM industry consultation is not that paper. Keep the absence statement explicitly bounded to the sources and date checked.
10. **OpenAI containment incident** — OpenAI’s 23 October post continues to support the incident timeline and the company’s description of its training controls. These are OpenAI’s own account and control description, not independent validation; preserve the article’s limits on what the public post establishes.
