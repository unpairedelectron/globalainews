The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) published an interim final rule, *Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors*, in the Federal Register on 19 November 2026. It took effect the same day. [1]

The core of it is a new end-use control. A licence is now required to export, reexport or transfer advanced chips when there is "knowledge" they will be used in a data centre — "wherever located" — that provides direct or indirect access to more than 12,000,000 "aggregate TPP" of computing power to companies headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent is headquartered in, Macau or a destination in Country Group D:5. [1] That group is the US arms-embargoed list, which includes China. [3]

Two details give the control unusual reach. The ceiling is measured across "linked data centers", which the rule says "includes digitally-linked infrastructure, not just physically-linked infrastructure". And a data centre that starts serving such customers *after* the chips arrive triggers a licence requirement on its own operator. Anyone already providing those services must obtain a licence within 90 days of the effective date "or face future export restrictions". [1]

The rule also adds a licence requirement for advanced chips going to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brazil and Thailand, which BIS describes as presenting an unacceptable risk of diversion. That requirement does not apply to end users whose ultimate parent is headquartered in the United States, provided the exporter files the reports set out in a new section 743.10. [1]

Separately, the rule formally rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule of January 2025. That removes the country tiers, License Exception AIA, the Data Center Validated End-User authorisation, and the export control on model weights, whose classification is deleted from the control list outright. [1] For the distinction between released weights and open-source licensing, see our explainer on [open weights versus open source](/article/open-weights-vs-open-source-ai/).

India is not named anywhere in the new controls. It sits in Country Group A:5 and does not appear in Country Group D at all. [3] Our explainer on the [IndiaAI GPU tender](/article/indiaai-10000-gpu-tender-explained/) covers the domestic compute route.

One oddity: the rule is written throughout in terms of "Super Intelligence" and "SI" rather than AI. That follows Executive Order 14434 of 29 September 2026, which directs the executive branch to use those terms "in place of" artificial intelligence and AI. [4] BIS's own announcement adds that the rule was "written by and at the direction of the Department of Treasury and the National Security Council". [2]

Comments are due by 19 January 2027. [1]
